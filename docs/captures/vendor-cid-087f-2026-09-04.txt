"capture start" 10:11:40, last data line 14:20:23, "capture end" 14:20:24.

HOW THE LINES WERE SELECTED
Every line carrying "adv=" was hex-decoded and walked as AD structures (length byte, type byte,
payload; stop on a zero length byte), and a line was kept only when a walked AD type 0xFF carried
0x7F 0x08 as its first two payload bytes, which is company ID 0x087F little-endian on the wire. A
substring search for "7F08" inside the advert hex returns 31 lines in drive_home_9-4.log; the walk
returns 27. The four extra are the reason the walk matters, and they are listed at the bottom of
this file: three are Apple 0x004C manufacturer payloads that happen to contain the byte pair, and
one is a 0x16 service-data structure under UUID 0xFCF1. None is a 0x087F device.

2. TWO ADVERT SHAPES, NOT ONE
The population splits cleanly, 12 devices each across the three logs.
  shape A  02 01 06 | 09 FF 7F08 0A 01 <4 varying> | 0C 09 "PCAM_" + last 3 MAC bytes in hex
  shape B  02 01 06 | 0E FF 7F08 00 00 00 <8 BCD bytes> | <len> 09 <name>
In drive_home_9-4.log that is 17 shape-A lines and 10 shape-B lines. The name rule holds for all 12
shape-A devices with no exceptions: the Complete Local Name is exactly "PCAM_" plus the last three
address bytes uppercase. No shape-B name restates its address. The shape-B name length varies with
the name, so the name AD length byte is 0x07 to 0x0C, and only the manufacturer structure is fixed
width there. The 8 trailing shape-B bytes are BCD-valid in every nibble for all 12 devices (16
digits) and always start 08. Their leading 8 digits cluster the devices into four runs: 08643950
(5 devices), 08669610 (4), 08608710 (2), 08618360 (1).
The vendor_capture.h row comment recorded shape A only and called the field shape "byte-identical in
every sighting" when this extract was written. Shape B is a second, longer manufacturer structure
under the same company ID. The header's FIELD SHAPE block has since been rewritten from this
extract: it now lists BOTH shapes with these byte sketches and states the bytes are NOT
byte-identical across sightings, so the two agree.

3. THE LENGTH BYTE SAYS FOUR VARYING BYTES, NOT THREE
Shape A manufacturer structure, in full: 09 FF 7F 08 0A 01 xx xx xx xx.
The AD length byte counts every byte after itself: 0x09 = 9. One of those is the AD type (0xFF), two
are the company ID (7F 08), two are the 0A 01 lead-in. 9 - 1 - 2 - 2 = 4. Four varying bytes follow
0x0A 0x01, and the raw lines below show four: 21 1A EE 51, 27 1B 22 5B, 1E 1B 17 3C and so on. The
whole shape-A advert is 26 bytes, 3 + 10 + 13, which is the 52 hex characters printed per line.
The vendor_capture.h comment wrote "<3 varying bytes>" when this extract was written. That was
off by one and the length byte is what settles it. The header's FIELD SHAPE block has since
been corrected and now sketches "<4 varying>" and says the 0x09 length byte settles the count
at FOUR, which is also what test_vendor_capture.cpp's PCAM fixture NOTE says, so all three
agree.
Those four bytes are also not constant per device. EF:19:4C:DE:76:BF advertised 0A 01 27 1B 67 16 at
483.0 s and 0A 01 28 1B 67 16 at 483.8 s in aug-9-drive2.log, 0.8 s apart. Byte 0 ranges 0x1E-0x2D
across the population and is the only one seen to move; bytes 2 and 3 were stable for every device
that was heard more than once. So the retired "byte-identical in every sighting" wording was wrong
even inside one device, which is why the header now says the bytes are NOT byte-identical across
sightings.

camarillo_drive and santabarbara contain no "[ble] ... adv=" lines at all. They were logged by the

this or any other advert. What they do have is name-only classifier lines, reproduced below: five
PCAM_ plus two CFQU BLE sightings in camarillo, one PCAM_ in santabarbara. camarillo also has a WiFi
SSID "PCAM26A" on a randomized MAC (de:22:79:3e:6b:b2). That is a WiFi SSID string, not a BLE
company ID, and it is not evidence about 0x087F.
The vendor_capture.h comment cited five logs for a shape claim "byte-identical in every sighting
across five drive logs" when this extract was written. Only three of the five contain any advert
bytes. The header's EVIDENCE BASE paragraph has since been narrowed to those three and now says
camarillo_drive and santabarbara "cannot support a byte-level claim", so the two agree.
Read counts as a floor, not a census. 171 "[ble] ... adv=" lines in drive_home_9-4.log are truncated
by serial drops and 9 more fail the AD walk, so 49,065 of 49,245 decode cleanly. None of the damaged
lines contains 7F08.

6. ADDRESS TYPE
All 24 addresses are BLE random-static: the top two bits of the first octet are set in every case.
Sorted, the 24 first octets are C6 C6 C8 C9 CB CC D2 D2 D3 DE E1 E2 E9 E9 EC EF F1 F3 F3 F9 FA FB FD
FE, and the lowest of them is 0xC6, so every one is >= 0xC0. The vendor_capture.h claim holds, 24 of
24.
There is no OUI in this population to look up, which is the point of carrying the company ID.

REGISTRY NOTE, NOT CAPTURE EVIDENCE
This paragraph is not derived from the logs. It comes from an external copy of the Bluetooth SIG
published company_identifiers list, where 0x087F (decimal 2175) resolves to Phillips Connect
Technologies LLC, a trailer-telematics vendor. The tree's own data is consistent with that: one
device in the same 0x087F population advertises PCTGW_83955, which reads as Phillips Connect
Technologies GateWay. Treat this as a lead only. Re-confirm it directly against the SIG
assigned-numbers list before the row is promoted anywhere out of capture, because nothing in this
repo holds a copy of that list to check against. vendor_capture.h said the registrant was unresolved
when this extract was written; the row comment, acab_version.h's 2.0.8 block, acab_scanner.h's
vendor-counter comment and docs/signatures.md have all since been updated to name Phillips
Connect Technologies with this same re-confirm caveat, so none of them still calls the family
unattributed.
Separately: "PCAM" is a vendor-chosen advertised name and is not evidence of a camera. A name string
costs a vendor nothing to pick, this project has already paid once for reading a product out of a
broadcast label, and trailer telematics is a better fit for the observed data than any imaging
device. The name is logged, not believed, and it must not enter a category decision.
